Notes

  • The circle of confusion (c) is the largest blur spot that still reads as a sharp point. A point outside the plane of focus is recorded as a small disc; while that disc stays smaller than c, it looks in focus. A smaller c is a stricter standard and gives less depth of field.
  • Hyperfocal H = f² ÷ (N × c) + f. Focused at H, everything from H ÷ 2 to infinity is within the circle of confusion c.
  • The pixel standard ties sharpness to the back: 2 × pixel pitch is a common choice for high-resolution files viewed large or at 100%. The classic diagonal ÷ 1500 standard suits a normal print at normal distance and is much more forgiving.
  • Diffraction uses the Airy disk diameter to its first dark ring, 2.44 × wavelength × N. Most of the light sits in a much smaller central peak, so this is a conservative early warning: softening comes on gradually, and sharpening recovers much of it at the first tier.
  • The Film standard is the film-era convention that lens depth-of-field scales are engraved for, scaled here to the sensor diagonal.
  • Maximum apertures, effective focal lengths and close-focus distances are Alpa's published figures for the current lenses in aperture units. The maths uses the effective focal length (the 40 is 41.85 mm). A different focusing mount or an older lens version can focus differently. Minimum apertures: f/32 for the 40 and f/45 for the 90 in Copal 0 shutters; the other lenses are shown to f/22 until their minimum is confirmed.
  • The 32 mm-marked HPF ring option applies only to Andrew's 40, which has the wrong HPF ring fitted; it uses his measured 1.16 ft (0.35 m) close focus.
  • Distances are measured from the lens's front principal point, so treat short distances as approximate. Helical scales may not match.
  • Not an official Alpa tool. Check your own setup before relying on these numbers.
